## Background

**On slide**
- The first competition for a supersonic strategic heavy bomber was launched in 1967.
- In 1972 a new competition called for a supersonic, variable-geometry bomber with a maximum speed of Mach 2.3, in response to the American B-1.
- The Tupolev design, named Aircraft 160M, incorporated(吸收) elements of the Tu-144 and competed against the Myasishchev M-18 and the Sukhoi T-4.
- The prototype flew on 18 December 1981, and production was authorised at Kazan in 1984.

The Tupolev 160M incorporated elements of the Tu-144 and beat the Myasishchev M-18 and the Sukhoi T-4, and the prototype flew on 18 December 1981, with production authorised at Kazan in 1984."

### Variable-Geometry Wing

**On slide — the calculation**
```
这里还是加上推导吧尤其是等效面积的推导，要求从头一步步推导
Aspect ratio(展弦比):            AR = b² / S
   wings forward:        55.7² / 400 = 7.76
   wings swept:          35.6² / 360 = 3.52

Wing loading at 275 t:   275,000 / 400 = 688 kg/m²   (forward)
                         267,600 / 360 = 743 kg/m²   (swept)

温度不要
```

- The outer wings sweep back from 20° to 65° to give high-performance characteristics at supersonic and subsonic speeds.
- Sweep costs aspect ratio, and induced drag rises as 1/AR, so the swept wing only pays when the lift coefficient is small.
- Titanium makes up around 30% of the 110 t empty weight, and the swing-wing hinge alone weighs six tonnes.

**Visual** Two planform outlines at 20° and 65°, with span dimensions; no Mach-number chart.

**Script** (101 words)
"The outer wings sweep back from 20 to 65 degrees to give high-performance characteristics at both supersonic and subsonic speeds, and the airframe is built around a titanium beam and an all-welded torsion box, with titanium making up around thirty per cent of the 110-tonne empty weight. Sweeping the wing costs aspect ratio, which falls from 7.8 to 3.5, so the swept configuration only pays when the lift coefficient is small, and at Mach 2.05 the stagnation temperature reaches about 125 degrees Celsius, which is one reason the structure is largely titanium and one reason the hinge alone weighs six tonnes."

**Source** Wikipedia (Design); Airforce Technology; own calculation, ISA at 12 km.

### Engines

**On slide**
```
Installed thrust:   4 × 245 kN            = 0.98 MN
Weight:             275 t × 9.81 m/s²     = 2.70 MN
Thrust/weight:      0.98 / 2.70           = 0.36

Drag at Mach 2:     D = W / (L/D) = 2.70 / 6 = 0.45 MN
Excess thrust:      0.98 − 0.45            = 0.53 MN
```

- Unlike the B-1B, which reduced the Mach 2+ requirement of the B-1A to achieve a smaller radar cross-section, the Tu-160 retained its variable intake ramps and Mach 2.05 at altitude.
- Four Kuznetsov NK-32 afterburning(加力) turbofans(涡扇), the most powerful engines ever fitted to a combat aircraft: 137 kN dry, 245 kN with reheat.
- The variable ramps(斜道) schedule oblique(倾斜) shocks(激波) and keep pressure recovery usable at Mach 2.
- The engines are sized for the dash(猛冲), not the cruise, hence the long runway and the low thrust-to-weight ratio.

**Script** (73 words)
"The aircraft has four Kuznetsov NK-32 afterburning turbofans, the most powerful engines ever fitted to a combat aircraft, each giving 245 kilonewtons with reheat for about 980 in total. Thrust-to-weight is only 0.36, but at Mach 2 the lift-to-drag ratio is about six, so drag at 275 tonnes is roughly 0.45 meganewtons and half a meganewton remains for acceleration and climb, with the variable intake ramps keeping pressure recovery usable at Mach 2."

**Source** Wikipedia (Design); Jane's; drag from D = W/(L/D).

**Script** (80 words)
"Weapons are carried in two internal bays, each capable of holding 22,500 kilograms of free-fall weapons or a rotary launcher for missiles, for a total weapons load of 45,000 kilograms — in practice twelve Kh-55, Kh-101 or Kh-555 cruise missiles with ranges of up to 3,000 kilometres. No defensive weapons are provided, because the Tu-160 was the first post-war Soviet bomber to lack them, and as a standoff missile carrier it survives by distance rather than by speed or stealth."

## History

**On slide**
`1981` first flight · `1987` service entry · `1992` production stops ·
`1999` Ukraine returns 8 · `2015` Syria debut · `2022` new-build M · `2024` in series

**16 August 2005 · Olenegorsk, Kola Peninsula**
Putin took the commander's seat for a five-hour flight of more than 5,000 km, with in-flight
refuelling, a supersonic run, and four Kh-555 missiles fired. All four hit an abandoned building

**22 February 2024 · Kazan**
Thirty minutes in a new-build Tu-160M, *Ilya Muromets*: "The technology is excellent. It really
belongs to a new generation. Of course, it can be accepted by the Armed Forces."

- Production stopped in 1992; Russia and Ukraine inherited 13 and 19 aircraft, and Russia later bought eight of the Ukrainian ones.
- The type made its combat debut in Syria in 2015, and the first newly built Tu-160M flew in 2022.

**Script** (68 words)
"Production stopped in 1992 after the dissolution of the Soviet Union, when Russia and Ukraine inherited thirteen and nineteen aircraft, and Russia later bought eight of the Ukrainian ones while the remaining eleven were scrapped under the Nunn–Lugar programme. The type made its combat debut in Syria in 2015, and in 2022 the first newly built Tu-160M flew, so the Kazan line had been restarted after twenty years."
**Script** (99 words)
"Two flights show the political life of the aircraft. On 16 August 2005, after the MAKS air show, Putin flew in a Tu-160 to Olenegorsk and took the commander's seat; during the five-hour flight, which covered more than 5,000 kilometres, the crews refuelled in the air, went supersonic and launched four Kh-555 missiles, all of which hit an abandoned building at the Pemboy range, two through the windows and two through the door. In February 2024 he flew for thirty minutes in a new-build Tu-160M from Kazan and said the technology was excellent and belonged to a new generation."

**Source** Wikipedia (Operational history); Airforce Technology.

## Challenges

**On slide**

| What the design buys | What it costs |
|---|---|
| Range and payload no other bomber matches | Radar cross-section you cannot hide |
| Supersonic dash, Variable sweep  | 6 t hinge(铰链),  3 km runway|
| Design experience from the Tu-144 | 1980s avionics: electromechanical cockpit(驾驶舱)|

- In the cockpit all the data is presented on conventional electro-mechanical monitors, and updating them is one reason the M version exists.

**Script** (73 words)
"The size that gives range also gives a radar cross-section that cannot be hidden, and the wing that gives Mach 2 costs six tonnes of hinge and heavy maintenance, while in the cockpit all the data is presented on conventional electro-mechanical indicators rather than head-up displays. The fleet is small, about seventeen aircraft, so each flight hour is expensive, yet the design remains coherent because it does one job better than anything else."

**Source** Wikipedia (Design); Airforce Technology.
